## Design Context

### Users
People at home in their living room, 2-4 m from a 1080p/4K TV, holding a D-pad remote. They glance at the screen between things: time, weather, a photo they like, then open Netflix, YouTube, Spotify or the PlayStation. Often the screen just sits there as a picture in the room. The project is open source, so the same layouts must work with anyone's apps, photos and devices.

### Brand Personality
Each theme has its own character; they share only quality and calm.
- Clock: a quiet gallery wall. The photo is the art, the time is a museum caption at large size.
- Glass: a premium device (Apple TV-like): depth, frosted surfaces, precise hierarchy.
- Postcard: warm and personal, like a printed photo on a fridge.
- Type: editorial, a magazine cover made of words.
Never: generic "AI dashboard", neon on dark, gradient text, glass everywhere, equal-weight clutter.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Photos dominate; UI must read over any photo (shades, shadows, never boxes for everything).
- 10-foot rules: nothing under ~18 px at 1080p for secondary text, focus visible from across the room, one focused thing at a time.
- Typography: avoid the reflex fonts (Instrument Serif, DM Serif, Space Mono, Inter, Fraunces...). Fonts must be OFL so they can ship in the APK.
- Reference the user liked: the original Glass Home weather panel (summary line + frosted 7-day panel).

### Design Principles
1. The photo is the hero; UI is a caption to it.
2. One clear focus: the focused tile must be unmistakable from the sofa; everything else recedes.
3. Each theme commits to one idea and executes it fully, instead of four variations of the same screen.
4. Consistency inside a theme: one icon treatment, one type pairing, one spacing scale (4/8/12/16/24/32/48/64 dp).
5. Real content only: real apps, real weather, real photos in every mockup.
